Working Papers

Intermediary Rents in Incentive Contracts [PDF]

R&R, American Economic Review

A principal hires an intermediary to incentivize an agent’s unobservable effort. Although the intermediary extracts rents, strengthening the agent’s outside option allows the principal to reclaim surplus. Consequently, the principal’s value and optimal contracts differ markedly from those in standard principal-agent models. Under intermediation, the principal’s value strictly increases over a range of the agent’s outside option and attains its maximum when that outside option is positive. Furthermore, equilibrium contract payments and effort are non-monotonic and discontinuous in the agent’s outside option. My results speak to welfare in labor markets and to the formation of contractual relationships within organizations.
